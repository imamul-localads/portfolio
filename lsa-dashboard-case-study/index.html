Your LSA Dashboard Is Disappearing

Your LSA dashboard has an expiry date. One morning it redirects to Google Ads — and three years of reports stay behind. Google gave you one email. Fourteen days. Here is the full written version: what moves, what dies on the way, and what you must save before your date comes.

Read it once, slowly — and save your data this week, before the email, not after it.

01One morning, your dashboard is gone.

Think of your business like a bucket. Leads are the water — you pour water in. The holes? That's where leads leak out. Now imagine someone moves your bucket to a new shelf — and half the water stays behind.

That is migration day. Your old LSA dashboard stops working. The page redirects you to Google Ads. Your history — what you spent, how many leads you got, your cost per lead — does not come with you.

Google sent you ONE email about this. Fourteen days before. You probably didn't read it. This is happening right now, and most business owners have no idea.

The takeaway: migration day has no undo button. When your date comes, the old dashboard closes for good.

02What is actually happening

So what is actually happening? Google is moving Local Service Ads INSIDE Google Ads. The new home is called Performance Max. Think of it like this — your shop stays in the same spot on the street. Customers still see you at the top of Google. But the building gets a new owner, with new rules.

The timeline: phase one started in August 2026 — plumbing, HVAC, roofing, electrical, house cleaning, all the big home services. Phase two comes later this year. And in 2027, the rest of the world follows. If you run home-service ads in the US, this is YOUR year.

The takeaway: LSA isn't ending. It's changing landlords — and the new landlord runs everything through automation.

03What dies on migration day

When your account moves, three manual controls die:

1.Manual bidding — goneYou can't set your own bids anymore. The machine decides.
2.Max cost per lead — goneThe cap you used to protect your budget disappears.
3.Targets per service — replacedInstead of one target for plumbing and another for HVAC, Google gives you ONE number for your whole account. One target cost per lead for everything.

And one more thing: you cannot opt out. When your migration date comes, it just happens. That one number is where the real problem starts. Let me show you.

The takeaway: one number for your whole account. That single target is where your best jobs start to leak.

04One number, two jobs — why the blended target hurts

Let me show you why one number is dangerous. Let's say you do plumbing AND heating and cooling. A plumbing job makes you, let's say, $500 profit. An HVAC install makes you $8,000 profit. Big difference, right? (These are example numbers — run your own.)

Now Google says: your target is $100 per lead. For plumbing, $100 is 20% of your profit. That's expensive. For HVAC, $100 is NOTHING — barely one percent.

05Your reports don't transfer — download them yourself

Here is the part nobody talks about. When you move, your history does NOT move with you. Your old LSA reports — how much you spent last year, how many leads plumbing got versus HVAC, your real cost per lead for each service — all of it stays behind in the old dashboard.

Google's own help page says it clearly: download it yourself, before the move. After migration day, that door is closed. Forever.

Three years of proof about what works in YOUR business — gone, unless you save it now.

The takeaway: the move can take up to two weeks and performance can wobble during it. Save your reports BEFORE it starts.

06The 14-day email — your only warning

So how do you know WHEN your move happens? Google emails the account admin. Fourteen days before. Then one reminder at seven days. That's it. No phone call. No big red warning. Just an email that looks like every other email Google sends you.

There is also a small banner inside the old dashboard — easy to miss if you don't log in every day. So here is what I want you to do, today: open your inbox and search for "Local Services." If that email is sitting there unread, your clock is already ticking.

The takeaway: fourteen days is not a lot of time. Check your inbox today, not when it's urgent.

07The six downloads — save these before the move

Before the move, save these six things:

1.Your spend historyEvery dollar, month by month. This is your baseline.
2.Your leads, split by servicePlumbing separate from HVAC. The split is the whole point — the new system blends them.
3.Your cost per lead, also by serviceWhat a plumbing lead really cost you versus an HVAC lead. You'll need this when the one target arrives.
4.Your bids and your targetsSo you remember what worked when you had control.
5.Your service areasWhere your ads actually ran and won.
6.Your ad schedulesWhich days and hours brought the calls.

Screenshot it, download it, email it to yourself — I don't care HOW you save it. Just don't lose three years of proof about what works. Do this BEFORE the 14-day email arrives, not during it. When you're rushing, you forget things.

The takeaway: any format works. What doesn't work is waiting.

08Three things to do this week

1.Find the email and mark your dateSearch your inbox for "Local Services." Put the migration date on a calendar where you'll see it.
2.Download all six reportsSpend, leads, cost per lead, bids, areas, schedules — the full checklist from section 07.
3.Split your services into separate campaignsPlumbing in one campaign, HVAC in another. Why? Because then each trade gets its OWN target number, and the robot can't mix your $500 jobs with your $8,000 jobs. This isn't my trick — it's Google's own workaround.
The takeaway: one campaign per trade. Write that down.

09Why Google is doing this — the honest answer

Why is Google doing this? Let me give you the honest answer. Google wants everything inside Google Ads, run by its AI. Manual knobs are disappearing everywhere — search, shopping, and now Local Services. Less control for you, more automation for them.

Now — I'm not saying the AI is evil. It CAN work well. But only if you feed it good data and set it up the right way. A robot with bad settings just loses your money FASTER. That's exactly why the checklist and the campaign split matter.

The takeaway: you're not fighting the machine — you're giving it better instructions.

10Find the hole. Fix the hole. Then grow.

Let's wrap it up. Three things, in this order.

Step one — FIND the hole. Your dashboard is moving into Google Ads, and your reports are not coming with you. Know your date.

Step two — FIX the hole. Save your data this week — before the email, not after it. And split your services so one target number can't starve your best jobs.

Step three — THEN grow. Only then do you scale.

Find the hole.Fix the hole.Then grow. In this order.

Your dashboard is moving. Your data doesn't have to die with it. I'll see you in the next one.
