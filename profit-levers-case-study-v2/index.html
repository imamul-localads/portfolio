Your Ads Aren't Broken.
Your Prices Are.

Most home-service owners don't have a lead problem. They have a price problem. Here are the 4 profit levers I find in every top operator — with the real math behind each one.

Here's what no ads guy will ever tell you: stop spending money on ads. Not forever — until you fix your prices. Because in business after business, I find the same hole. The owner is underpriced. And because he's underpriced, he has no margin.

1The trap: no margin, no room

Look at the industry numbers. The average shop keeps five to ten percent. The struggling ones keep two to five percent. Out of every hundred dollars that comes in… some owners keep TWO.

No margin means you can't afford a bad month. You can't afford a good hire. You can't afford to test new marketing. One truck breaks down — one transmission — and you're back in credit card debt.

So what does he do? He works harder. More jobs. Lower prices to "stay competitive." And the hole gets deeper. I tell every owner the same thing: doing more of what doesn't work… will never work.

The trap: underpriced jobs mean no margin, and no margin means you can never afford the things that would help you grow.

2The 4 profit levers

I dug into what the top operators in the window and home cleaning industry actually do. Not theory — real businesses, real numbers. And it always comes down to 4 levers.

1
Price — what you charge.
2
Average ticket — how much each job is worth.
3
Frequency — how often they buy again.
4
Referrals — who they send you.

Small move on each lever… big jump in profit. Here is the math I show every owner.

3Lever 1: Price — the $100K → $30K math

Let's say a cleaning business does one hundred thousand dollars in a month. Profit is ten percent — right at the industry average. So the owner keeps ten thousand.

Now raise prices twenty percent. Same jobs. Same costs.

LineExample
Revenue$100K → $120K
Costs$90K → $90K
Profit$10K → $30K

Look at that. Twenty percent higher price… THREE TIMES the profit. That's not greed. That's oxygen. That extra twenty thousand is what buys better trucks, better staff, better marketing. In every business I study, profit is what lets them grow.

My honesty rule: if your net margin is under ten percent — don't buy more ads. Fix your pricing first.

And here's how you know your prices are too low. If you're closing more than half your quotes… your prices are too low. Raise them until you start hearing "no." You'll hear more no's — and make more money anyway.

4Lever 2: Average ticket — make each job worth more

Don't just raise the price — raise what each customer BUYS.

I studied a window cleaning company that charged four dollars a window. Today they charge fifteen. Same windows. Same work. Almost FOUR TIMES the money per job.

I studied a pressure washing business that took its average ticket from six hundred to eighteen hundred dollars. They didn't raise prices. They changed HOW customers buy — packages. Good, better, best.

Say your average ticket is one thousand dollars. Make it fifteen hundred. That's fifty percent more revenue per job. Same number of jobs.

How? Say the right words first — tell them the value before you name the price. Then cross-sell: they called for window cleaning? "Want us to do your gutters while we're here?" Then upsell — sell them MORE of what they asked for. Bigger package. Better option.

And sell packages — three of them — and show the HIGHEST price first. Always. Why highest first? Because of anchoring. When they see the big number first, the middle package looks reasonable. Even if they never buy the top one… you make more money. Every time.

Three-package rule: good, better, best — highest price shown first. The middle package becomes the easy yes.

5Lever 3: Frequency — sell to the same customer twice

I look at the numbers and most cleaning businesses get one visit a year per customer. Get them to twice a year… you just doubled that customer. Same ad spend. Zero extra marketing.

How? Reminders. Maintenance plans. One simple text:

TEMPLATE · THE 6-MONTH REMINDER

"Hey, it's been six months since your last cleaning. Want me to book you in?"

Why it works: you paid good money to win that customer — ads, quotes, driving out there. This text brings them back for free.

A maintenance plan flips the whole game. They pay you every quarter, automatically. No re-selling. No chasing. Most owners I study never ask twice — so the customer forgets they exist.

6Lever 4: Referrals — the cheapest leads on earth

After every job, ask. "Who else do you know who needs this?" One sentence. Most owners never say it.

And partner up. Find five non-competing businesses — a plumber, an electrician, a roofer, a cleaner, a landscaper. Send each other customers. You don't pay for ads. You pay with trust. One good partner can feed you jobs every single month.

And there's a referral that works while you sleep: Google reviews. Every 5-star review is a stranger telling your next customer to trust you. Ask for it the same day — that's when they're happiest.

Three referral doors: ask every customer, partner with five non-competitors, collect Google reviews the same day.

7The lie in your head

So why doesn't everyone do this? Because every owner I talk to has the same lie in his head.

"I'll lose customers if I charge more." "That's price gouging." "Rich people are greedy."

Listen. Nobody is forcing anyone to buy. You name your price. They say yes or no. That's business.

Here's what studying the top operators taught me. You don't need ten new books and fifteen new systems. You need to DELETE one lie from your brain.

Delete "I'm scared to charge more" — and everything changes.

8Price is perception

And one more thing I see across every top business. Price is not just a number. It's PERCEPTION.

They did a test. Same wine. Poured from a heavy bottle — people said it tastes better. Poured from a cheap box — people gulped it down. Same wine. Different experience.

Your business is the same. How fast you answer the phone. How you greet them. Your uniform. Your quote. Your follow-up. When the experience feels premium… the price feels fair. When the experience feels cheap… every dollar feels expensive.

Watch any market. The 4.9-star company with two hundred Google reviews charges more than the 3.2-star one with twelve. Same service. Different perception. Reviews are what let you raise your prices without apology.

So don't just raise your price. Raise the EXPERIENCE around it. And here's the beautiful cycle: higher prices give you the margin to deliver better service. Better service gets better reviews. Better reviews bring more referrals. Price feeds everything.

9Recap — pick ONE lever this week

Find the hole.Fix the hole.Then grow.

I'm a marketer. I look at numbers all day. And the numbers say the same thing every time. Four levers. Price. Average ticket. Frequency. Referrals.

You don't need all four today. Pick ONE. This week. Raise one price. Add one upsell. Send one reminder text. Ask one happy customer for a referral. Small lever… big profit.
