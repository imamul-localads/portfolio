The $2 Customer Is
Killing Your Business.

Here's something that changed how I look at every business. Half the people out there have $2. The other half has $98. And most owners spend all their energy fighting for the $2 half.

1The $2 / $98 split

This isn't about literal dollars. The $2 customer is the bargain hunter — the one who haggles, compares five quotes, and picks the cheapest. The $98 customer is the value buyer — she pays full price and judges you on the work, not the discount.

Every local market has both. But most owners build their whole business around the $2 half: discounts, deals, "best price guaranteed." Today I'll show you the real numbers behind both — and why the cheap ones are killing your business.

2The whale curve

Researchers studied a real company. They looked at every single customer and asked one question: who actually makes us money?

The top 20% of customers made 225% of all the profit. The bottom 10% destroyed 125% of it. Only 4 out of 10 customers were profitable at all.

And here's the crazy part: the two BIGGEST customers were among the LEAST profitable. Big doesn't mean profitable.

The whale curve: a small slice of your customers makes all your money — and a small slice at the bottom quietly eats it.

3Cheap customers rate you worse

Researchers studied 7 MILLION Yelp reviews. Customers who came through Groupon deals rated businesses half a star lower — 3.27 versus 3.73 for normal customers.

The cheap customer doesn't just pay less. He rates you WORSE. And one star on Yelp is worth about 9% of your revenue.

So the discount didn't just cost you the discount. It cost you the next ten customers who read that review.

Think about it: you paid to acquire a customer who paid you less — and then told strangers not to come.

4They complain more, cost more

Researchers at the University of British Columbia found bargain hunters are 18% more likely to file complaints.

And Sprint — the phone company — once FIRED a thousand customers. Why? They were calling 40 to 50 times a month. A normal customer calls less than once. Each call cost Sprint 10 to 20 dollars.

Do the math. That's a customer who costs you hundreds and pays you tens.

The line to remember: the cheap customer isn't cheap. He's EXPENSIVE.

5Customer A vs Customer B

Let's say you quote an $800 cleaning job.

Customer A haggles you down to $650. He calls three times with complaints. You spend an extra hour fixing things. He leaves 3 stars. He never comes back. After your time and the headache — you kept almost NOTHING.

Customer B pays your full price — $1,200. No haggle. She leaves 5 stars. She rebooks twice a year. She sends you her neighbor. One customer becomes FIVE jobs.

CustomerLifetime value
A — $650 once, gone$650
B — $1,200 × rebooks × referrals$6,000+

Same work. Different customer. Different business.

6It works in the real world

One landscape company shared their real numbers. They quit all their low-margin mowing work. Overtime dropped 35%. Company profit went from 35% to 47%.

They did LESS work and kept MORE money.

And Jobber's data — thousands of cleaning businesses — shows revenue growing because invoice sizes are going UP, five to eight percent. Not more jobs. Bigger tickets.

The pattern: smart pricing, not more jobs.

7Fish where the fish are

Moody's measured this across America: the top 10% of earners do almost HALF of all consumer spending.

And the government's own numbers for home repair: the richest fifth spends 41% of all the money. The bottom fifth spends 9%.

41 versus 9. The money's already there. You're just fishing in the wrong half of the lake.

8"But Walmart!"

Now someone will say — but Walmart wins on low prices! Yes. And Vanguard wins on low fees.

But Walmart does 681 BILLION dollars at 2% margins. Vanguard holds 11 TRILLION. Low price only works with MASSIVE scale — or a product that costs nothing to serve.

You have five trucks. Not five thousand stores. You can't run their playbook. So stop trying.

9The fix

1
Raise your prices — until the $2 customers leave ON THEIR OWN.
2
Remember my rule — if you're closing more than half your quotes, your prices are too low.
3
Earn the $98 customers — better service, better reviews, better experience.

You don't fire the $2 customers. Your prices do it for you. Then you earn the $98 ones — the ones who pay full price, leave 5 stars, rebook, and send their neighbors.

The goal: fewer jobs. Better jobs. More profit.

10Find the hole. Fix the hole. Then grow.

The hole was never your ads. It was WHO you were selling to.

Find the hole.Fix the hole.Then grow.
