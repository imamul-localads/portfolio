If you are an advertiser, if you are a business owner — this one is for you. From October 1st, 2026, a missed call doesn't just cost you the customer anymore. Now you lose the customer AND you pay Google for a call you never answered.

This article is the full written version. Read it once, slowly — and you will never look at a ringing phone the same way again.

01The phone rings. You don't answer. Google bills you.

Think of your business like a bucket. Leads are the water — you pour water in. The holes? That's where leads leak out. A missed call was always a hole. But from October 1st, 2026, that hole got more expensive.

Before, a missed call cost you the customer. Now it costs you the customer plus a lead fee on your Google bill. Same hole. Bigger leak.

The takeaway: a missed call is no longer just a lost customer. It is now a direct cost on your Google bill.

02Missed call = paid lead

So here's the change, simple. Before October 1st: customer calls, you miss it, you lose the customer. That's it. After October 1st: customer calls, you miss it, you lose the customer — and Google charges you for the lead anyway.

Why? Because from Google's side, that call WAS a lead. A real person searched, saw your business, and called. Google delivered what you paid for. Whether you picked up is your problem now.

The takeaway: Google sells the call, not the conversation. Answering is your job.

03What LSA actually is

LSA means Local Service Ads. When someone searches "roof repair near me," the box at the very top of Google — with business names, star ratings, and review counts — those are Local Service Ads.

Here is the key part: you don't pay per click here. You pay per lead. Every phone call from that box is a lead you bought. That's why a missed call can now show up on your bill — you already bought the call the moment the customer dialed.

The takeaway: LSA is pay-per-call, not pay-per-click. The meter starts when the phone rings.

04The new rule, exactly

Let me break it down. Three conditions. All three must be true before Google bills you:

1.The caller stays on the line more than 20 secondsIf they hang up before 20 seconds — you're safe. No charge.
2.Nobody answersNo pick-up, no callback in time — the call goes nowhere.
3.It happens during your business hoursMidnight call while you're closed? That shouldn't be a billable lead — which is why your hours must be correct in your settings.

And one detail people miss: if you have a phone menu — "press 1 for sales" — the 20 seconds only start counting AFTER the caller presses a button. The menu time doesn't count.

The takeaway: more than 20 seconds + no answer + business hours = you pay. Miss any one of the three and there's no charge.

05The money math

Let's do the math. Let's say one LSA lead costs you $53. (That's an example — your lead cost will be different. Run your own numbers.)

Now let's say you miss 10 calls in a month. 10 × $53 = $530.

That is $530 — for zero jobs. Not $530 that brought you work. $530 for conversations that never happened.

06LSA is moving into Performance Max

Now, there is a second big change you need to know about. Google is moving all of LSA into Google Ads — as something called a Performance Max campaign.

It's still pay-per-lead. But everything underneath changes. And when the system changes, things disappear.

The takeaway: the billing change and the platform move are happening together. Prepare for both, not one.

07Three things you lose on migration day

When your account moves, expect three losses:

1.Your old reportsHistorical performance reports don't move into Google Ads. Download them first — Google emails you 14 days and 7 days before your migration.
2.Manual cost-per-lead biddingThat bidding option is going away in the new setup.
3.Weekly budgets as you knew themBudgets become daily averages. Same money, different rhythm — watch your spend the first two weeks.
The takeaway: the move can take up to two weeks and performance can wobble during it. Save your reports before it starts.

08Why Google is doing this

Ask yourself: why would Google charge for a call nobody answered? Because speed wins. Google wants every caller connected to a business. A business that answers in 10 seconds is a better result than one that never picks up.

So Google is using the bill to push behavior: answer the phone, or pay for the silence. The businesses that survive this change won't be the ones with the biggest ad budget. They'll be the ones with the best systems.

The takeaway: systems beat budgets. The fix isn't more ad spend — it's fewer missed calls.

09Six things to do this week

All right — what do you actually do about it? Six things, this week:

1.Open your LSA settings and check your business hoursWrong hours = paying for calls you were never open to take. Fix this first.
2.Set up missed-call text-backMissed the call? An automatic text goes out in seconds: "Sorry we missed you — how can we help?" The lead stays warm.
3.Push your answer rate toward 95%That's the target the top local advertisers aim for. Every unanswered call is now a line item.
4.Reply to messages within 30 minutesLSA isn't just calls — message leads count too. Fast replies keep them billable in the good way: they become customers.
5.Get fresh reviews every monthFive new reviews a month keeps your star rating alive — and the rating is why customers call you instead of the next listing.
6.Track every callWhere it came from. Did you answer. What happened. The paper doesn't lie — and now the bill doesn't either.
The takeaway: do these six before you spend one more dollar on ads. Plug the hole, then pour more water.

10The follow-up machine

Here's my framework, the one I use: the 5-minute callback. You miss a call? Five minutes. That's the rule. Not tomorrow. Not "when I'm free." Five minutes.

Why five? Because after five minutes, the customer is already calling your competitor. The lead you just paid Google for is dialing someone else.

Answer the phone. Missed it — call back in 5 minutes, then follow up: call, text, call, text, call. Nobody slips through.

The takeaway: the 5-minute callback turns a paid missed call back into a conversation — before the customer moves on.

11Find the hole. Fix the hole. Then grow.

Let's wrap it up. Three steps, in this order.

Step one — FIND the hole. Missed calls are now a direct cost. Measure yours. Count them this week.

Step two — FIX the hole. Answer rate up, callback system on, hours correct, text-back working.

Step three — THEN grow. Only then do you scale your ads.

Find the hole.Fix the hole.Then grow. In this order.

Your ads are pouring the water. Now fix the hole they're pouring through. I'll see you in the next one.
